Task 6

Do you have any idea that might explain these differences biologically? Consider, that the reefs in Moorea were completely destroyed in 2010 due to an outbreak of Crown-of-thorns starfish, a coral predator, followed by a cyclone in 2010. The recovery afterwards until 2019 was very fast compared to other reefs. We also know that the cyclone hit the North and West harder than the East of Moorea. Why could this have been an advantage for the recovery at those sites?

What other factors might have impacted the recovery after 2010?

Afficher l’indice

It could be that the recovery was faster in the North and West than in the east because the cyclone cleared the substrate and made space for new recruits. At the outer reef in Moorea, we currently have the problem that there are many dead skeletons left after the 2019 bleaching event that break apart in the swell. This is a very unstable substrate for new coral recruits and may hinder recovery. No cyclone occurred after the 2019 bleaching event. So before, the faster recovery in the North and West of Moorea might have let to the higher coral cover observed at these sites.

But of course also other factors may have impacted coral recruitment and cover, as for example sedimentation, nutrient input, herbivorous fish biomass (coral compete with algae for space, algae remain more common when there are less herbivorous fish).
